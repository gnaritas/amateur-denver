Why “Amateur”?

Someone who does it for the love of it

The word traces to the Latin amare, “to love.” An amateur, literally, is someone who does something out of love — the reframe two former software people chose for their first restaurant.

Chef & co-owner

David Arnold

Executive sous chef at Uchi for many years; helped open the Japanese kaiseki chef counter inside Odell’s Bagels. His own project, Freehand Provisions, began as a line of hot sauces built from excess farm produce food banks wouldn’t take — and grew into a popular series of family-style farm dinners and private dining, up to a one-month residency in this year’s Up NXT cohort at BearLeek in RiNo.

Trained by the kitchen, educated by the farm.

Co-owner & wine educator

Hannah Cunningham

Opened Bon Vin near Sloan’s Lake about a year ago after chef stints including Margot (while it was a pop-up at Noisette) and her own micro-bakery, Mother Sauce. She hosts the tastings, pairing classes and food pop-ups outside Bon Vin that have brought chefs like Monarch’s Justin Freeman, Cimera’s Geoff Cox and Farang’s Ben Whelan to the neighborhood.

A wine shop is a classroom that happens to sell wine.

How they found each other

A chef needed a front-of-house. A front-of-house needed a chef.

They met through Denver’s Up NXT pop-up series — Hannah the first year, David the next — and discovered they were separately searching for the same thing: a space for a neighborhood bistro with great food and fairly priced, serious wine. “It was kind of serendipitous.”

Both left careers in software — she as a designer, he as an engineer. The name is the joke and the thesis at once.

The press on the debut
